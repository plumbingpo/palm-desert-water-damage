Appliance Leak Water Damage Help in Palm Desert

Three appliances cause most of the leaks we see. When the supply hose on a washing machine splits, water flows under full pressure until somebody turns it off. With a dishwasher the leak is typically slow, coming from the door seal or the drain hose where the cabinets hide it. A refrigerator ice maker line is a thin tube that can drip behind the unit for months. No two of these call for the same cleanup.

On every Palm Desert job, the first step is moving the appliance and tracing the path the water took. In kitchens that usually means under the cabinets and beneath the flooring. Laundry room water goes into the wall at the back of the machine and the neighboring rooms, and with an upstairs laundry, the ceiling underneath. We extract what is standing, measure what has soaked in, and set drying equipment sized to the wet area.

Do This First

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceCancel the cycle, and pull the plug only when the floor and the outlet near it are dry. Otherwise, use the breaker to cut its power.
  2. Close the Supply ValveFor a washing machine, close both the cold and hot valves on the wall at its back. The dishwasher valve is under the kitchen sink. A refrigerator line has a small valve below or behind the unit.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterUse towels or a mop on whatever water is within reach, which keeps it from spreading beneath flooring and cabinets. Use a vacuum only if it is a wet and dry model.
  4. Leave the Door ShutIf the washer or dishwasher has not drained, leave it shut. Opening the door mid-cycle can release the whole load of water onto the floor.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartPhotograph the failed fitting, valve or hose, and hold on to the part itself. Your insurer and the appliance maker may both ask to see it.
Our Process

Our Appliance Leak Water Damage Process

1

Pull and Inspect

The appliance is disconnected and moved, and the supply is confirmed off. Then thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went below flooring, under the cabinets and into the walls nearby.

2

Extract the Water

Carpet and floors are cleared of standing water with extraction equipment. To reach water caught below cabinets or under floating floors, our crew pulls toe kicks or takes up boards.

3

Save Materials by Drying

We set air movers and dehumidifiers and, where possible, dry cabinets and hardwood in place with directed airflow so they do not have to be torn out.

4

Sanitize and Take Final Readings

We apply antimicrobial treatment wherever discharge from the washer or the dishwasher made contact. Final moisture readings are taken before we return the appliance, so it never sits on a damp floor.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • A dishwasher with a failed pump seal, a cracked drain hose or a worn door gasket.
  • Too much detergent or an overloaded drum that makes the machine overflow.
  • The drain hose of the washer pulling free of the standpipe, or the standpipe backing up from a clog.
  • A dishwasher that was installed out of level or with a supply connection left loose.
  • Ice maker lines, copper or plastic, that crack, kink or come loose where they connect.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Flooring and Subfloor

Water slides beneath laminate, vinyl plank and hardwood. The boards swell or cup, while the subfloor below remains wet well after the top looks dry.

Baseboards and Wall Bottoms

Drywall edges and baseboards pull up water that stands on a kitchen or laundry room floor. Often the worst of it is on the wall in back of the appliance, where it went unseen.

Rooms Next Door

For hours, a failed washer hose can keep releasing water. It passes under walls into hallways, closets and bedrooms, and soaks carpet and pad well away from the laundry room.

Mold Behind Appliances

Hardly anyone looks at the dark, warm space behind a dishwasher or refrigerator. Mold on the floor and wall can be fed by a slow drip in that spot well before it is noticed.

Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Appliance Leak Water Damage

When a machine overflows unexpectedly or a hose bursts, that is typically treated as sudden and accidental, which many homeowners policies cover. What is typically paid is the water damage, while the failed part and the appliance are left out. A claim is harder when an ice maker line or dishwasher seeped slowly, since coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks. Certain insurers will want to know the hose's age and whether it was kept up. Hold on to the part that failed, photograph everything ahead of cleanup, and review what your policy says.
